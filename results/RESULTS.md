# Results

Generated by `ctb reproduce` from the committed team outputs. Study dates: 2026-11-02 to 2026-11-05. Model: claude-opus-5-5 (every assistant message in every transcript, except one follow-up launch on Claude Sonnet 5.5 that is reported apart).
Harness: Claude Code 2.1.318 subagents, one per team, each in its own workspace; isolation by instruction, audited.

Standard errors in parentheses, in percentage points. *n* is the number of packets or criteria.

The control cited worse than the study's two earlier trials on the same packets (entry F1 0.496 vs 0.572, more bank statements added). Against those trials instead of the control, the examples close 0.45 of the gap (not pre-registered).

With the examples, entries that only add documents almost disappear (85 to 9), and so do entries that add some and leave out others (32 to 8). Entries missing one of the key's documents fall less: 112 to 90 (22 fewer, 95% CI 1 to 43; not pre-registered), against 15 to 55 per code team on the same packets. With the examples, the documents most often missing from entries that only leave documents out are `work_order` 15, `delivery_note` 12, `customer_invoice` 9.

## 4. Effort (from the transcripts)

| Kind of run | Runs | Tool calls per run | Output tokens per run | Cache-read tokens per run |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| apex judge | 4 | 8 | 5,378 | 454,423 |
| apex team | 4 | 37 | 44,315 | 4,217,276 |
| direct answer | 31 | 15 | 21,363 | 560,547 |
| finbalance team | 8 | 49 | 72,428 | 7,021,118 |

Answering directly costs about 3,619 output tokens and 94,956 cache-read tokens per packet, every time. A code-writing team spends about 72,428 output and 7,021,118 cache-read tokens once; its `run.py` then answers all 143 hidden packets in under a second with no model calls. On output tokens alone the break-even is roughly 20 packets (cache reads per packet depend on how many packets each agent answered, so they are not compared on their own).

At Claude Opus 5.5 list prices (input, cache writes, cache reads and output; 5-minute or 1-hour cache writes), one code-writing team cost $3.85 to $4.45 and answering directly $0.17 to $0.22 per packet, so one program pays for itself after about 20 to 22 packets. The study ran 8 teams ($31 to $36), more than answering the 143 hidden-A packets once ($24 to $31); the practice rounds that shaped the scaffold are not counted.

## Reading these numbers

* **Fully right:** writing code is ahead by +5.7 points, 1.8 standard errors once run-to-run variance between teams is counted (95% CI [-0.4, +11.8]). Suggestive, not established. Chosen after the fact: without the two v7 teams +5.9 [-0.7, +12.6]; no-preparation teams only +7.5 [+1.8, +13.3].
* **Citations are the largest difference, and checked practice closes it:** entry F1 with document references 0.57 answering directly vs 0.91 writing code. Answering directly, every citation shares at least one document with the key's (0 entries cite only other documents). It adds documents the key does not cite (299 entries; the bank statement in 294 of the 320 extra documents), leaves out one or more of the key's documents (265 entries), or both (68). Both arms read "support-only docs may also be included when needed"; only the code teams had 30 solved examples showing which documents the key expects, and only they were told that citations are scored. Counting entries that cite at least the key's documents, the gap is +0.18 [+0.15, +0.22]. A pre-registered follow-up (section 1b) gave the direct answer three solved examples: on 40 packets its entry F1 with doc refs rose by +0.22 [+0.17, +0.28], closing 0.56 [0.42, 0.69] of the gap to the code teams. Entries that only add documents fell from 85 to 9; entries missing one of the key's documents fell less, from 112 to 90 (code teams 15 to 55). The pre-registered rule calls a share of 0.5 or more "most of the gap"; the interval, and 0.45 measured against the study's own direct trials, support "about half", which is how the README reads it. What the examples fixed least is the documents the direct answer leaves out. A second pre-registered follow-up (section 1c) separated the causes on the same packets: notes the agent wrote itself after 8 practice packets checked by the scorer closed 0.90 [0.77, 1.02] and 1.03 [0.95, 1.10] of the gap in two chains, and cut entries missing one of the key's documents from 111 to 39 and 21; the three examples, repeated, closed 0.57 [0.41, 0.71]; being told that citations are scored closed 0.00 [-0.25, 0.19], and Anthropic's journal-entry-prep skill -0.05 [-0.25, 0.10]. What closes the gap is checked practice, not code and not knowing the scoring. In the public FinBalance release every record (1,706 of 1,706) has a bank statement and 0 of 18,704 key entries cite one. The FinBalance authors report document-reference mismatches on 52% of expected postings for Gemini 3 Flash on their 143-record compact split; their evidence-only condition, which drops every document no key entry cites, lowers that by 26 points.
* **Scaffold:** two teams per scaffold arm, so only large effects are detectable (team-to-team SD pooled over the three arms, t interval). Scaffold v7 was written after seeing hidden-A errors, so for v7 only hidden B is a clean test: -0.3 points against no preparation [-7.8, +7.1]. The scaffold was delivered as files the brief told teams to read (`CLAUDE.md`, a protocol, skill and agent files); the teams ran as subagents and made no subagent or skill calls, so what was tested is a written method, not Claude Code's skill and hook machinery.
* **Isolation:** agents were isolated by instruction and every transcript was audited (`results/isolation_audit.json`); see docs/threats-to-validity.md for what the audit found and what it cannot see.
