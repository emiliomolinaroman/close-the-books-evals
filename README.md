[![ci](https://github.com/emiliomolinaroman/close-the-books-evals/actions/workflows/ci.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/emiliomolinaroman/close-the-books-evals/actions/workflows/ci.yml)

When Claude does month-end close work on data written by accountants, is it better to have it
**write a program that computes the answer**, or to have it **read the documents and answer
directly**? And does a prepared scaffold (a `CLAUDE.md`, a protocol, and skill and subagent files
the team is told to read) help the team that writes code?

This repository holds the experiment, everything the agents produced, and `ctb`, a small
package that rebuilds the data splits, re-runs every submitted program on hidden data and
recomputes every number below with standard errors.

## Results

Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code, November 2026. Two public benchmarks written by people, not by
an LLM: [FinBalance](https://github.com/Devansh1105/finbalance) (packets of OCR'd documents;
the answer is the journal entries with citations and an exact balance sheet, or a contradiction
code) and the [APEX-Accounting](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mercor/apex-accounting-harbor)
dev set (ten close requests for a law firm, graded by rubrics written by accountants).

## What the evidence supports

1. **The citation gap comes from learning from checked practice, not from writing code.**
   Answering directly, Claude finds the right accounts and amounts (entry F1 0.91 without
   citations), but its citations match the key less often (0.57). It adds the bank statement as
   corroboration, which the task statement allows ("support-only docs may also be included when
   needed") and the key never does, or it leaves out one of the key's documents. The code teams
   had 30 solved examples to check their program against; the direct agents had neither examples
   nor checks. Two pre-registered follow-ups on 40 of the packets separate the causes:
   * three solved examples close about half of the gap: 0.56 (95% CI 0.42 to 0.69) on
     6 November, and 0.57 (0.41 to 0.71) when repeated on 8 November in another runtime;
   * notes the agent wrote itself after 8 practice packets checked by the scorer close all of it:
     0.90 (0.77 to 1.02) and 1.03 (0.95 to 1.10) in two independent chains, entry F1 with
     citations 0.86 and 0.91 against 0.90 for the code teams;
   * being told that citations are scored, which only the code teams' brief said, closes none of
     it (0.00, -0.25 to 0.19), and neither does Anthropic's `journal-entry-prep` skill installed
     as shipped (-0.05, -0.25 to 0.10), although every session called it.

   Examples mainly teach which documents not to cite (entries only adding documents 71 to 5).
   The checked practice also fixes the documents the direct answer leaves out: entries missing
   one of the key's documents 111 in the control, 83 with examples, 39 and 21 with the notes, 15
   to 55 per team for the programs.
2. **On whole packets the gain is modest and not yet established.** Fully right packets: +5.7
   points for code; 95% CI +0.2 to +11.1 holding the eight programs fixed, and -0.4 to +11.8
   counting run-to-run variance between teams. An earlier 40-packet comparison suggested +13;
   extending the direct-answer arm to all 143 hidden packets, because a power calculation said 40
   was too few, halved it. Answering directly was slightly better at naming contradictions (87%
   vs 85%) and on the easiest packets. In the follow-up, whole packets with three solved examples
   moved -2.5 points (95% CI -11.1 to +6.1): 40 packets are too few to say anything about that.
   With the self-written notes it moved +12.5 and +15.0 points (95% CIs +2.1 to +22.9 and +3.8 to
   +26.2), to 82.5% and 85.0% against 82.8% for the code teams on the same packets. The
   pre-registration calls fully right underpowered at this size, so nothing rests on it; it is
   the next test to run on all 143 packets.
3. **No evidence that the scaffold helps on clean, human-written data, and no power to rule out
   a small effect.** On hidden split B, the only clean test for v7 (it was revised after reading
   hidden-A errors), the two v7 teams scored 87.3% and the four teams without preparation 87.7%:
   -0.3 points, 95% CI -7.8 to +7.1. v6 was 4.7 points lower on both hidden splits, mostly
   because of one team. What was tested is a written method the teams read: no team called a
   subagent or a skill. On APEX the scaffold team scored +1 to +2 criteria out of 89.
4. **Code is also cheaper at volume.** At list prices, counting cached input as well as output,
   one code-writing team cost about $4 and answering directly about $0.17 to $0.22 per packet:
   one program pays for itself after about 20 packets. Answering with the notes costs the same per
   packet as without them ($0.15 in the second follow-up), after a one-off $3 to $4 per chain of
   practice.

What this does *not* show: anything about messy real-world documents (FinBalance packets come
from templates, which is part of why parsers work); whether the notes would transfer to packets
of other companies or conventions (they were written on and tested against one benchmark); or how
accountants would grade the APEX answers (both judges were Claude). The two follow-ups ran in
other runtimes than the main study (subagents on Claude Code 2.1.316; then `claude -p` on
2.1.319), so each is compared only with its own control, run at the same time. The 6 November
control cited worse than the study's own direct trials (0.50 vs 0.57); against those trials the
examples close 0.45 of the gap. See
[threats to validity](docs/threats-to-validity.md).

## Quick start

```bash
pip install -e ".[dev]"
ctb fetch finbalance --data data          # pinned commit, checksums verified
ctb reproduce --data data --work work     # re-runs all 8 programs on 293 hidden packets, rescoring everything
```

`ctb reproduce` rewrites `results/summary.json`, `results/RESULTS.md` and the per-record
outcomes in `results/per_record/`; CI fails if they differ from what is committed.

Score your own program or predictions:

```bash
ctb finbalance build --data data --out work/finbalance
ctb finbalance score --split hidden_a --program path/to/run.py          # sandboxed, no network, 600 s
ctb finbalance score --split public --predictions predictions.json
```

Run a new team with the Claude Agent SDK (workspace rules enforced in code; not a security sandbox):

```bash
pip install -e ".[agents]"
ctb run-team work/teams/A1 --prompt prompts/agent_briefs/finbalance_team_no_prep.md --model <model-id>
```

Other commands: `ctb apex build` (team workspaces), `ctb apex summarize` (blind verdicts,
clustered SEs, judge agreement), `ctb audit` (transcript isolation audit and token usage),
`ctb verify` (dataset checksums). `ctb --help` lists everything.

## How it was run

* **Teams.** Each team was one Claude Code agent alone in its own workspace with the task
  statement, 30 solved examples and 60 unsolved public packets. It had to deliver a `run.py`
  that answers new packets with no network and no model calls. 8 FinBalance teams in three arms,
  4 APEX teams in two. In the experiment the public ids spelled out the contradiction codes, and
  seven of the eight teams say they checked their detectors against those ids; no `run.py` reads
  them and hidden ids were neutral.
* **Hidden evaluation.** Every `run.py` was executed on two hidden splits generated from another
  seed (143 and 150 packets), in a sandbox with networking disabled.
* **Direct answer.** 31 agents read the packets as text and wrote each JSON answer by hand, with
  no shell, code or calculator: all 143 packets once, 40 of them twice.
* **Follow-up (6 November).** The same 40 packets again, 8 agents with the trial-2 brief and 8
  with it plus three solved examples, all started together on Claude Opus 5.5; question, rule
  for reading the result and two deviations in
  [`experiments/2026-11/followup_examples/`](experiments/2026-11/followup_examples/).
* **Second follow-up (8 November).** The same 40 packets in six arms, one headless Claude Code
  session (`claude -p`, Claude Opus 5.5) per group and arm, driven by a harness that fixes the
  model, the tools, the turn limit, the settings sources and the MCP servers on the command line;
  a read outside a session's folder is denied by the CLI. Two memory chains of 8 practice packets
  each, with feedback from the scorer, wrote the notes the memory arms read. Pre-registration,
  harness, logs and the notes in
  [`experiments/2026-11/followup_teaching/`](experiments/2026-11/followup_teaching/).
* **APEX grading.** Two blind Claude judges, the second with the answer labels swapped. They
  agreed on 177 of 178 criterion verdicts.
* **Statistics.** Paired differences with standard errors, task-clustered SEs for rubric
  criteria, bootstrap checks, team-level SEs for arm comparisons, a power calculation.
* **Audit.** 95 transcripts (47 from this study, 48 from the practice rounds) checked tool call
  by tool call: no tool call names answer keys, other teams' folders or raw data. The audit sees
  absolute paths only. The 32 transcripts of the two scored follow-up launches were checked the
  same way, and stricter: every path any tool call names is inside the agent's own folder. The
  80 sessions of the second follow-up were audited from their stream-json transcripts: every
  assistant message from Claude Opus 5.5, only the listed tools, no path outside the folder, no
  permission denial.

## Repository layout

```text
src/ctb_evals/          the package: splits, scoring, sandbox, stats, APEX judging, audit, report, agent runner
tests/                  pytest suite on synthetic data (no dataset download needed)
prompts/                task statements (Spanish originals, English versions), judge prompt, every agent brief verbatim
scaffold/en, scaffold/es  the Claude Code scaffold tested (v7), in English and in the original Spanish
team_outputs/           what the agents delivered: programs, notes, public predictions, direct answers, APEX answers
results/                summary.json, RESULTS.md, per-record outcomes, judge verdicts, isolation audit, runs.csv
experiments/2026-11/    study definition, the 40-packet sample, the original scripts as run, practice-round notes,
                        the two pre-registered follow-ups
docs/                   methodology, eval card, threats to validity
```
